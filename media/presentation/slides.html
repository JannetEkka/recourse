The problem

An algorithm decides who keeps subsidised food

Telangana's system matches people across government databases. A “match” removes the family from the scheme. It tagged Bismillah Bee's late husband, a rickshaw puller, as a car owner, and her family lost its rations.

Al Jazeera with The Reporters' Collective, January 2024

The family can't see which record did it. Nobody outside the department can check which rules ran, or whether they changed. The department keeps the only log, and it is the party being checked.

20 lakh

ration cards deleted in Telangana from 2016 on

The South First

Who it's for, and what it does

The family

needs to know what decided its case, in its own language.

The legal-aid worker

files the appeal and needs to know exactly which record to dispute.

The auditor or journalist

needs to see how often each version of the rules is wrong.

  1. Rules are published on-chain before they're used. The contract refuses decisions made under rules that weren't public first.
  2. Every decision gets a receipt listing only the records that decided it.
  3. Gemini explains the receipt in Telugu, Hindi or English, checked by a guard.
  4. Appeals and the agency's answers go on the record, with a deadline anyone can see.
Technical architecture

Decisions stay in the agency's system. Only hashes cross to the chain.

Three design choices

What keeps it honest

Privacy

No personal data on-chain

Only hashes of salted receipts, one Merkle root per batch. The receipt is the family's own.

Reasons

Only records that decided it

A record is a reason only if removing it changes the outcome, so a refusal can't be padded with records that didn't matter.

AI

Gemini rewords, it can't add

A guard checks every number and record ID against the receipt and rejects any draft that invents one or leaves out a decisive record.

Python, Solidity and the browser compute the same hashes byte for byte. 21 contract tests and 21 engine tests run on every pull request.

What's new

Accountability the agency can't edit

  1. A quiet rule change becomes a public, dated event. Rules are committed before use, and each receipt says whether the earlier rules would have decided differently.
  2. Reasons are tested, not listed. The flip test comes from my trading agent SMT, where an advisor's reason is shown only if flipping its input changes the decision.
  3. The AI explains but can't add facts.
  4. A missing answer is visible to everyone, with a date.
Social impact and feasibility

Impact

For families who lose food through a database match they can't see, and for the legal-aid groups who help them appeal.

In the demo, the change to v2 caught 17 more real cases and wrongly cut off 117 more families. The public record shows it through appeal outcomes alone.

Feasibility

The agency changes one step: it seals each batch and publishes each rule version. About 160,000 gas per batch of 1,005 decisions.

Decisions stay in the system it already runs. A pilot starts with a legal-aid group that already files ration-card appeals.
